Scalable Graph Classification via Random Walk Fingerprints

Peiyan Li (LMU Munich)
Honglian Wang (KTH Stockholm)
Christian Böhm (University of Vienna)

An unsupervised, interpretable, and linearly scalable graph feature extractor. RWF partitions every graph's vertices by structural role (hubs V1 vs. periphery V2), computes multi-step random-walk connection strengths via sparse matrix powers Tτ, and feeds a compact, size-invariant fingerprint directly to a Support Vector Machine.

Comparative Landscape of Graph Classification Methods

Method Family Core Mechanism Complexity (Table II) Attributes Empirical Outcome
Score: 0 / 0
01 · Problem

Classify whole graphs (molecules, proteins, social networks). Kernels require O(N²) pairwise comparisons and GNNs demand heavy training.

02 · Core Idea

Fingerprint each graph by how strongly its structural role groups (hubs vs. periphery) connect via multi-step random walks.

03 · Dimension

d = τc·(k²/2 + 3k/2) (+ 2kp with features). Only 30 numbers for k=2, τc=6 — completely size- and permutation-invariant.

04 · Empirical Result

Best on 8/12 TUDatasets; 6–10× faster than NetSimile/NetLSD; FGSD remains faster on 5/9 (particularly dense small graphs).

Strengths (§5.2)

  • Linear O(N) scaling; handles 127,094 graphs (TWI) where kernel baselines time out or OOM.
  • Directly interpretable coordinates (e.g., hub-to-periphery connectivity at 3 steps).
  • Unsupervised sparse matrix powers; reusable with any downstream classifier.
  • Principled node-attribute integration via P and Q matrices; supported by Fig. 2 ablation.

Limitations & Open Questions (§5.2)

  • Dense graphs degrade speed due to m·n term (TWI: 2,631s vs. FGSD 43s).
  • No formal theoretical proof of expressive power (explicitly acknowledged by authors).
  • Small accuracy margins on small datasets, frequently within 10-fold standard deviation.
  • Equal-size degree chunks break ties arbitrarily; best k, τc chosen per dataset; mixed MATLAB/Python timings.
